# Writing clearly and concisely

## Overview

William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style* (1918) is the sentence-level
standard for this repository. It teaches one habit: make the sentence clear,
then cut everything that does not help it.

The full reference is `elements-of-style.md`, about 12,000 tokens. Read it
when you are writing or editing more than a short passage. Do not carry it
for a task that only needs the rules below.

## When to use this skill

Use it for any prose a human will read:

- documentation, README files, and technical explanations;
- commit messages, pull request descriptions, and review comments;
- error messages, UI copy, help text, and code comments;
- reports, summaries, and explanations;
- editing or rewriting someone else's text.

If you are writing a sentence for a person to read, use this skill.

## The rules at a glance

### Elementary rules of usage

1. Form the possessive singular by adding `'s`.
2. Use a comma after each term in a series except the last.
3. Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas.
4. Use a comma before a conjunction that introduces a coordinate clause.
5. Do not join independent clauses with a comma.
6. Do not break a sentence in two where the second half is a fragment.
7. A participial phrase at the beginning of a sentence refers to the
   grammatical subject.

### Elementary principles of composition

8. One paragraph per topic.
9. Begin the paragraph with a topic sentence.
10. Use the active voice.
11. Put statements in positive form.
12. Use definite, specific, concrete language.
13. Omit needless words.
14. Avoid a succession of loose sentences.
15. Express coordinate ideas in similar form.
16. Keep related words together.
17. Keep to one tense in summaries.
18. Place emphatic words at the end of the sentence.

### Words and expressions commonly misused

Section V of `elements-of-style.md` is an alphabetical reference. Open it when
a word choice is uncertain; do not guess from memory.

## Working with a tight context

When the context window is tight:

1. Write the draft with these rules in front of you.
2. Read the draft aloud in your head and cut what you would not say.
3. Ask a fresh reviewer, or the caller, to copyedit with the full reference.

Do not skip the review because the reference is large. Load it for the edit,
then let it go.
